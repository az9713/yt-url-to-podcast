Anthropic Engineers Just 10x'd Everyone's Claude Code

Anthropic reports that it removed over 80% of the Claude Code system prompt for its newest models with no measurable loss on its evaluations. The video applies that finding to a personal setup of CLAUDE.md files and skills, and shows two audits that find what to cut.

The claim

The presenter says an Anthropic engineer published an article on engineering for the Claude 5 generation of models. Per the video, the team deleted more than 80% of the Claude Code system prompt for Opus 5, Fable 5 and 5.1, and later models, and saw no measurable loss on its evaluations. The presenter's reading is that the older advice, to load Claude with as much context as possible, now slows the model down.

He ties this to a remark by Boris Cherny, who created Claude Code: about every six months, delete all of your skills. The mechanism he describes is rule drift. Claude makes a mistake, you add a rule, and months later the rule exists in CLAUDE.md, in a skill, and in a slightly different copy elsewhere, while the model has been upgraded and no longer needs it.

The presenter adds caveats. The paper tested older models in controlled experiments, so its percentages do not predict what happens when you trim your own CLAUDE.md. The MCP result is stronger support, because Claude kept access to the tools and only stopped loading all their instructions up front. Anthropic's applied AI team also says "minimal" does not mean "short": a detail the model needs stays in.

His own rule of thumb: the best context is the smallest amount that gets the highest quality at the lowest cost. One example of an old rule that Anthropic replaced: a hard cap on code comments gave way to "follow the style of the surrounding code."

The presenter's experiment

He duplicated his personal AI operating system and removed CLAUDE.md and the skills in the copy. Both versions turned an interview with Boris Cherny into a student resource guide. The full setup produced the more polished document, with his branding, heading and links. The stripped version looked plainer but organized the interview into ideas, added timestamps, and read as a better guide.

His conclusion is to keep the look in a skill (header, font, footer, logos) and give the model only the intent for the content. His old skill contained guardrails written for Opus 4, and they confined a model that no longer needed them. He compares this to handing an experienced designer a first-presentation checklist.

Keep: what Claude cannot infer or look up, such as voice, goals, audience, how the business works, and the reason behind a request. Cut: facts it can look up, such as a long folder listing; the same instruction repeated in several places; and procedures that apply to one task, which can load only when that task runs. CLAUDE.md stays as a starting point: what the project is for, known gotchas, and pointers to deeper instructions.

Audit 2: a workflow prompt

His prompt: "Audit this repo and show me the smallest change that would make the path from a raw YouTube idea to a filming ready script faster. Use the systems already here. Cite the exact files you would reuse and do not edit anything yet."

Claude mapped idea, research, outline, script and teleprompter file. It found a handover gap. The outline skill could gather facts but did not require saving the evidence in a file the script skill reads, and the script skill had a hard rule to verify facts again. The audit counted 25 video folders with a script and only three with a file named sources.md. The presenter notes this shows an inconsistent handover and does not prove the other videos lacked research. Claude proposed three edits across two skills, and he approved them.

Cadence and automation

He suggests running such audits monthly if the setup changes often, quarterly if it is stable, and each time you switch to a new model, since each model reads settings, skills and instruction files a little differently. Once the audits are trusted, a scheduled local task in the Claude desktop app could run /doctor monthly and leave recommendations to approve or deny. His aim is a self-cleaning AI operating system, because human consistency is the weak point, with team syncing as the next step.
